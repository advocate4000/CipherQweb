HNDL — Harvest Now, Decrypt Later

Intelligence agencies and advanced persistent threat actors are collecting and archiving encrypted traffic at scale. Any data protected by classical asymmetric cryptography today will be retrospectively decrypted the moment quantum hardware is capable.

CRQC — The Cryptographic Event Horizon

A Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computer with ~4,000 stable logical qubits can execute Shor's algorithm to factor RSA keys and solve discrete logarithms. NSA and NIST both project this capability arriving between 2030 and 2035.

NCSC 2028 — The Deadline That Applies To You

The NCSC expects UK organisations to have completed cryptographic discovery and drafted a migration plan by 2028, high-priority migration by 2031, and full transition by 2035. It is guidance rather than law — but it is a published reference point with dates attached, which is what boards, auditors, insurers and enterprise customers now ask questions against. Discovery is the part almost nobody has done. US National Security Systems fall under NSA CNSA 2.0 on a comparable arc.

Harvest Now, Decrypt Later is the axis every briefing covers. It is not the only one, and it is not the one that will be hardest to explain afterwards. A quantum-capable adversary can take confidentiality, authenticity, or access — and the three behave nothing alike once they have.

The move Traffic recorded today is read once a CRQC exists. A recovered signing key mints new trusted artifacts. Genuine credentials are presented. Nothing is forged, so nothing looks wrong.
Detectable No — the capture is passive, and it happens somewhere you cannot see. Yes — a forged artifact exists, and can be examined. No — nothing is anomalous. The audit entry is clean.
Retired by Nothing. Captured traffic stays captured. Re-issue under post-quantum signatures. Rotation.

The last row is the one that matters. HNDL and DNEL both get worse with delay, but they do not get worse in the same way. A credential recovered from a five-year-old handshake is worthless if the key it authenticates has since been re-issued. Traffic captured five years ago is not worthless, and never will be. That is why CipherQ scopes operator access around rotation and gateway placement rather than around cryptography alone — and why a DNEL finding is the one your logs will never raise.

204
NIST FIPS 204

Module-Lattice Digital Signature Algorithm (ML-DSA), based on CRYSTALS-Dilithium. The primary NIST standard for post-quantum digital signatures.

Commercial National Security Algorithm Suite 2.0. Binding NSA guidance for National Security Systems mandating migration to ML-KEM and ML-DSA with hard deadlines.
